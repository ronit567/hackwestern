Operating-room early warning

Warns the anaesthetist minutes before blood pressure falls

Every 30 seconds during surgery, the model reads the signals already on the anaesthesia monitor and estimates the chance that arterial pressure (MAP) falls under 65 mmHg for at least a minute in the next 5, 10 or 15 minutes. The goal is more time to act.

    Limitations

    • Retrospective and from one hospital (VitalDB, Seoul National University Hospital). It needs external validation.
    • Patients with an arterial line: a fixed-seed sample of adult general-anaesthesia surgeries, sicker than average.
    • Hypoxemia (low SpO2) had too few events to test, so no claim is made about it.
    • Not built yet: the trajectory model (GRU and 1D CNN on the arterial wave) and the pattern model. These results are the XGBoost snapshot model plus fusion.
    • Vasopressor infusions are nearly absent from VitalDB and boluses have no timestamps.
    • Alarm reasons are SHAP drivers of the snapshot model in plain-language templates, not causes.

    It shows more time to act before the pressure falls, from signals already on the monitor. It does not show that patients do better.
